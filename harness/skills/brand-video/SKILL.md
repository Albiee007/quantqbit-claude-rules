# Brand Video

Make short videos that look and move like **this project**: the backgrounds, type, accents and motif of the approved direction, animated with the project's own motion tokens. Every frame is an HTML page seeked to an exact time, so a render is repeatable and reviewable frame by frame.

## Inputs
- The creative direction and its approvals (`direction.py status`). Video needs Gate 1 (direction), Gate 2 (a video concept) and, per piece, Gate 3 (the storyboard), all recorded together at one owner checkpoint ([autonomy](../creative-direction/references/autonomy.md)).
- Motion tokens in the project's token file (`$type` duration and cubicBezier). `render_video.py motion-tokens` suggests them from the mood; `--write` adds them without overwriting.
- What the video is for: platform, length, message, call to action, voice-over. Take them from the request, then `families.video.brief`, then the listing and README. Where all are silent: `social-9x16`, 15 s, the listing's call to action, silent video plus the voice-over script. State these as assumptions; never stop to ask for them.

## Workflow
1. **Direction.** No video family requested, or no motion tokens: the creative-director adds `families.video` (with a brief) and runs `render_video.py motion-tokens`. Requesting a family no longer makes other approvals stale.
   **Reference and method.** When the owner supplies a visual reference, or the piece needs people, places or objects the templates don't draw, write `brand/video/<id or series>/visual-plan.md` before the concept round, following [visual-production](references/visual-production.md). It records what the reference requires (appearance, character actions), the owner's constraints, the method chosen (code illustration, procedural or modelled 3D, generated art composed in code, supplied footage) and its limits. Without a stated constraint, choose the method yourself: masters already in `brand/art/` or supplied footage first, then code illustration and templates; generated art only with the owner's go-ahead for the paid calls, offered at the checkpoint. The pilot sample is the checkpoint draft, not an extra stop. When the owner asks, compare methods on the same shot.
2. **Concept round.** `video-creative` (concept mode) writes 2–3 video concepts (`direction.py concept new --family video`) per [concept-round](../creative-direction/references/concept-round.md): genuinely different motion and pace, all in the approved look. Each gets a preview (`render_video.py preview <piece> --concept <file>`): a sheet of each scene's still frame and a half-size draft. The creative-director critiques and recommends one; the storyboard is drafted on it.
3. **Storyboard.** `render_video.py init <id>` writes a starter `brand/video/<id>/video.json`. Fill the scenes (template, duration, copy, `vo` line) from the brief and the project's real claims, and list every format the piece is for. A format that needs a different layout or shorter copy gets a scene's `byFormat` entry (layout, copy, hide); timing is never per format. Run `render_video.py lint <id>` until it reports no problems, then `preview <id>` (one folder per format; `--format` for one).
4. **One owner checkpoint.** The owner watches the draft, reads `voice/script.md`, sees the recommended concept against the alternatives and the triaged lint items, in one message. A yes records the direction, the concept, the storyboard (and the mix, when there is audio) in one chained command: `direction.py approve --gate direction,concept,storyboard --family video --concept <file> --piece <id> --by ... --evidence ...` (main session only; `direction.py status --next` prints it). When the owner delegated, the main session records it with that evidence and renders.
5. **Render.** `render_video.py render <id>` checks the gates, lints, renders every frame of every format, encodes, makes the loops and posters, checks, writes `brand/runs/video/<run>.json` and then publishes to `brand/video/<id>/out/`. A FAIL publishes nothing. `--engine hyperframes` lets the pinned HyperFrames CLI draw the frames (our encoder and checks, plus a parity signal). `--workers N|auto` renders in parallel browsers; the file is the same whatever the number (the render checks the frames where their runs meet).
6. **Reviews.** REVIEW REQUIRED items (sampled contrast, safe areas, flashing, reading time, voice-over fit, loop seam, file size, picture provenance and resolution) are listed in the run manifest. The output is published but not cleared for use until the owner looks and the main session records it: `direction.py approve --gate review --run <run> --items <ids|all> ...`. Look at every item yourself first and say in the final report what you checked; the owner's look then needs one reply. `render_video.py status` shows what is open. A byte-identical re-render keeps the acknowledgement.
7. **Voice and music.** Hand `out/voice/voice-prompt.md` and `script.md` to whoever makes the voice (a voice actor or any TTS tool). When the takes come back, put them in `brand/video/<id>/media/vo/<scene>.wav`, add an `audio` section (voice, music with its licence, loudness), `preview` to hear the draft mix, and the owner approves the mix: `direction.py approve --gate mix --piece <id>` (main session only; chained with the storyboard when both are new: `--gate storyboard,mix`). The next render mixes, normalises and checks the sound, and the captions follow the takes. See [audio-and-voice](references/audio-and-voice.md).

## Templates and vocabulary
- Scene templates: `title`, `feature` (head plus up to five points), `stat` (a number and its label), `end-card` (logo, call to action, URL), `still` (a picture from the project, `cover` or `contain`, with an optional slow `push-in`, `pull-out` or pan and an optional caption), and `recording` (a screen recording from `mobile-screen-capture`, `media.video` from `media.from` seconds, shown frame by frame with an optional caption). The concept lists which a project uses; `custom` scenes are the project's own HTML and script, animated with Web Animations or a project-supplied library (a paused GSAP timeline, a Lottie animation) the seek positions; see [composition-contract](references/composition-contract.md). A custom scene can compose the project's own images (layers, plates, inserts) through `custom.assets`, which the script reads as `ctx.assets`. `still`, `recording` and `custom` are opted in by the concept. A picture not made for the project needs `media.license`; its bytes are part of the storyboard approval.
- Layouts `type-start`, `type-center`, `type-lower`; transitions `cut`, `fade`, `dip`, `slide`, `push`, `scale`, `wipe`; text entrances `fade-up`, `mask-up`, `word-stagger`, `scale-in`, `fade`. A scene may use only what its concept allows; anything else needs an owner exception (`--scope "video:<piece>:scene:<scene>:<kind>=<value>"`).
- Formats: `social-9x16` (1080×1920), `social-4x5` (1080×1350), `social-1x1` (1080×1080), `wide-16x9` (1920×1080) and `og-card` (1200×630, an animated link card). A piece up to 15 s can add `loop` outputs (GIF, animated WebP) and a `poster` still per format; `kind: "loop"` pieces get a GIF by default and the loop-seam signal. `app-preview-iphone` (886×1920) and `app-preview-ipad` (1200×1600) are App Store previews: 30 fps, 15–30 s, a sound track, mostly recordings (graphics scenes are a suitability review item). `transparent: true` makes an overlay with an alpha channel (`alpha`: `webm` or `prores`). Google Play takes a YouTube link: use `wide-16x9`. See [formats-and-profiles](references/formats-and-profiles.md).

## Rules
- **The look comes from the concept,** never from the piece: no colours, fonts or CSS in `video.json`. Change the look through the concept and its approval.
- **Generated media only on request.** `render_video.py generate --what tts|align|broll` runs the project's own provider commands (`brand/video/providers.json`; keys stay in the environment), only from the main session after the owner's go-ahead (asked at the checkpoint, never assumed, also in delegated mode). Generated files are recorded in provenance and listed for AI disclosure. See [providers](references/providers.md).
- **The owner decides** concepts, storyboards, mixes and review items, once, at the checkpoint (or by delegating); agents never record approvals and never call a paid or external voice or video service.
- **Truthful copy.** On-screen claims and voice-over lines follow the same claims guardrails as the store listing; no invented numbers (a `stat` scene needs a real figure).
- **Artwork quality comes from the method, not the renderer.** The renderer composes, moves and checks what it is given. Match the method to the reference and prove it on a sample before scaling ([visual-production](references/visual-production.md)). Pictures come from full-size masters: `lint` flags a still the format has to enlarge.
- **Reading time and pace are signals.** `lint` reports scenes too short to read or lines too long for their scene; fix them or say why they stay, and the owner decides at the checkpoint. The concept's minimum hold is enforced.
- **Accessibility:** no more than three flashes a second (the render's flashing heuristic is a signal; look at anything it flags), keep text inside the safe insets, ship the caption sidecar with the video where the platform takes one.
- **Use the scripts.** Don't write your own render, encode or check scripts; don't hand-edit `out/`, `.build/` or run manifests.
- **ffmpeg** must be installed (`render_video.py` names the install command per OS); Chrome, Chromium or Edge renders. Media files never go into `.claude/`.

## Output
- `brand/video/<id>/video.json` (project-owned), `brand/video/<id>/out/`: per format `<format>/<id>.mp4` (`.webm` or `.mov` for overlays), `<format>/<id>-sheet.png` (each scene's still frame) and, when asked for, `<id>.gif`, `<id>.webp`, `<id>-poster.png`; once per piece `voice/script.md`, `voice/voice-prompt.md`, `voice/cues.json`, `voice/captions.planned.srt|vtt` (or `voice/captions.srt|vtt` when they follow recorded takes); `brand/runs/video/<run>.json`.
- A report: concept and storyboard approvals, the check table, open review items, and the voice hand-off.
